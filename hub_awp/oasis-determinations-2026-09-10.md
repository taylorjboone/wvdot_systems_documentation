## 1. The document chain

**D-1 — A professional-agreement payment is a sequence of four OASIS documents,
not one.** [PAG]

```
  procurement document           per-invoice order      payable       payment
  ────────────────────           ─────────────────      ───────       ───────

  APO                      ──►   (none — the APO   ──►   IN      ──►   PRM or PRC
  Agency Purchase Order           is itself the          to A/P        generated by
  prefix PAG + fiscal year        order)                               OASIS,
  prefix ROW for right-of-way                                          approved by
                                                                       the Auditor,
  AMA                      ──►   ADO              ──►   IN      ──►   then paid
  Agency Master Agreement         Agency Delivery        to A/P
  prefix PAG                      Order, prefix PAG
                                  one per invoice
```

Section 20, verbatim: *"After the delivery order is approved, the Division/District
must work with Accounts Payable to process an IN. Once the IN is approved, a PRM
or PRC will be generated and once approved the vendor will receive payment."*

**D-2 — PRM/PRC is generated by OASIS. Nobody keys it.** [PAG]

This is the determination that matters most for the portal, because the portal
currently models the payment document as the thing a person keys from a
screen-ready layout. The documents a person actually creates are the **ADO** and
the **IN**. The payment document is downstream of both and is produced by the
system.

Tony and Jake described the same thing in the room, roughly: *"as these invoices
come in, they have to create an IN. And then the IN generates a PRM automatically
for the payment. So there's two more documents. Well, three, actually, because
there's the IN, the payment, and then the actual payment."* [Room]

**D-3 — One AMA per agreement. One ADO per invoice. Not one per project.** [PAG,
correcting Room]

In the meeting the model was stated as *"one AMA would end up with, like if there's
25 projects, you'd have 25 AMAs… and then for every project, it's going to have
another AMA."* Christy flagged it on the spot — *"it's kind of more complicated
than that, and I have a document that walks through all that stuff"* — and
Section 20 settles it:

> *"The ADO will allow for the Division/District to incorporate the appropriate
> number of accounting lines needed to split the invoice across multiple projects."*

So the project split lives on **the ADO's accounting lines**, one ADO covering one
invoice across however many projects. The portal's existing allocation rows are
already shaped exactly this way.

Also settled: *"After the AMA is approved, a delivery order must be issued and is
required to process a payment."* No ADO, no payment.

**D-4 — Which document type is decided by the shape of the agreement.** [Room,
confirmed by PAG]

| Agreement | OASIS document | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| One project, one funding source | **APO** | *"anything that's just got a single project is always going to be an APO"* |
| Multiple projects, single vendor | **AMA** + an ADO per invoice | *"if it's a master agreement, it would be AMA"* |
| Right-of-way | APO with a **ROW** prefix | [PAG] — mentioned once, see Open-1 |

Residual uncertainty from the room, now resolved by the documentation: *"You said
it had to be AMA. Is that right? Well, that's where I get confused. We'd have to
ask Tony. Because, like, if this is one of those CEI things, it is an AMA. So it's
the AMA, and then you do your ADO for each of the invoices."*

**D-5 — GAX is the legacy path, not a live option.** [PAG]

The open question of months — GAX or PRC — is answered by the worked example in
Section 20. An AMA approved for $134,000 shows $53,585.82 *"previously paid though
REMIS and via the GAX process"*, brought into OASIS by a catch-up delivery order
(`ADO PAG24*02`) purely to account for money already paid. `ADO PAG24*03` is the
first invoice actually processed through the OASIS procurement path.

GAX belongs to the mainframe era. It does not need to be modelled going forward.

**D-6 — Delivery orders under an agreement are discoverable in OASIS.** [PAG]

Document references → Forward Reference lists every ADO issued under an AMA, each
one a hyperlink to the approved document. Useful both as a reconciliation surface
and as a read target if API or scrape access is ever granted.

## 2. The boundary: what the portal will and will not do

This was the most deliberate set of determinations in the meeting, and the
boundary was stated more than once in the same words.

**D-7 — The portal walks to the gate. A person goes through it.** [Room]

> *"I can get you all the way to the gate of OASIS. Whatever you need to stand at
> that gate and hit yes as soon as possible, I would like that… I can walk you all
> the way up to the gate. I just can't take you to the gate. But I can pre-populate
> the OASIS gate for you."*

The portal never presses submit. This is a standing constraint, not a phase-one
limitation.

**D-8 — The fill-forward mechanism is browser automation acting as the signed-in
user.** [Room]

> *"Technically, right now, in a browser, as long as they have the screen open, you
> can tell it to write to that screen — you just can't hit the button for them to
> submit."*

> *"It would have to be logged in as Jake Bumgardner, but then he doesn't have to go
> retype in [the numbers]. It will do that on his behalf. OASIS is not going to
> realize it's the computer that's doing it… So he's going to have to be responsible
> for that. But if he's approved that and signed it, it can do the front-end data
> entry. It just can't interact with OASIS."*

Named technology: Playwright. The accountability model is explicit — the person
whose session it is remains responsible for what gets entered, which is why the
in-portal signature (out of scope here, but coupled to this) has to come first.

**D-9 — A buffer sits between the portal and OASIS. Bennett called it the lily
pad.** [Room]

> *"We're building a lily pad between us and OASIS. And we can feed the lily pad,
> and every now and then the lily pad will update OASIS. Similarly, updates from
> OASIS can hit the lily pad and then come back to us. If you query OASIS, it's
> going to filter it all through the lily pad."*

The rule worth writing down is the netting:

> *"So if you had $100 to spend, and you knew that 10 were spent — the lily pad had
> 10 waiting in there — even though there's 100 in OASIS, you're going to see 90,
> because you've already put 10 in the lily pad."*

Available balance = OASIS balance − commitments pending on the portal side. The
stated purpose: *"that way we're minimizing our interactions with OASIS, so we
don't have to get a recertification, and we're still getting current data."*

**D-10 — Double entry is the accepted interim state.** [Room]

> *"until they tie to OASIS, we will be doing double entry. But what would be
> amazing, if we didn't have to do that. We can give you all the data to put into
> the front of OASIS."*

Nobody pretended otherwise. The portal's job in the interim is to make the second
entry as close to mechanical as possible.

**D-11 — The blocker is on the OASIS side, and it is a year out.** [Room]

> *"But what's the holdup? No new interfaces. Well, they've got to get their upgrade
> in place. They have an upgrade that's launching next month or next year. It's next
> year now."*

So the integration asks — attaching documents, a real interface, a data feed — are
not refused on merit. They are queued behind the OASIS upgrade. Worth re-raising
when that ships.

## 3. Money: encumbrance and fiscal years

**D-12 — The encumbrance is split across fiscal years on the accounting line, by
event type.** [PAG]

| Event type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PR05** | The commitment will be fully paid in the **current** fiscal year |
| **PR07** | The commitment falls in the **next** fiscal year. One additional PR07 line per subsequent year |

Section 20: *"The Division/District is required to determine if the commitment will
be fully paid in the current fiscal year. If so, the entire agreement amount is
recorded as an encumbrance PR05 with the current fiscal year. However, if the
agreement is expected to cover multiple fiscal years, the Division/District must
split the remaining financial commitment over multiple fiscal years."*

**D-13 — The fiscal-year lines must sum to the agreement total, exactly.** [PAG]

Twice, in both documents. Section 20: *"The total agreement must equal all the
fiscal years recorded in the accounting line for the document."* Section 22: *"All
of the accounting lines need to equal the total contract amount on the Commodity
Screen. If they do not add up, you will get an error."*

This is a second tie-out rule, independent of the project-split tie-out the portal
already enforces. It is computable from data the portal already holds — term dates
and the ceiling — so the portal can catch it before OASIS does.

**D-14 — The encumbrance liquidates when the IN is approved.** [PAG]

From the worked example: *"Once the IN/CEC is approved, the encumbrance of
$53,585.82 will be liquidated and will display as the total amount expended under
the agreement."* This is the OASIS-side counterpart to the portal's billed-to-date
figure, and the two should be reconcilable.

**D-15 — Changing the money or the term needs a Procurement change order. An
accounting adjustment does not.** [PAG]

Section 20: *"If the agreement is extended or if the agreement amount [is] increased
or decreased, the Division/District will need to work with Procurement to process a
change order to modify the agreement."*

Section 22 adds the mechanics, including one easily-missed step:

- **Always uncheck "Track Changes"** when processing a change order.
- The reason starts with "Change Order" and the number, and says what changed.
- *"If you are modifying the fund accounting, just indicate Accounting Adjustment.
  You do not need to create a Change Order for an accounting adjustment."*

Practical consequence: a supplemental's ceiling is not usable for payment until
that change order is approved. Procurement is the gate.

## 4. Keying: the field values are now known

**D-16 — Section 22 documents the accounting and header values for a professional
services agreement.** [PAG]

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Fund | usually **9017** |
| Department | **0803** — Highways |
| Unit | **0060** — Engineering |
| Appropriation Unit | varies — **not captured** (the source page is an image; see Open-2) |
| Object | **8202** Consulting Agreement, or **3285** Government Subrecipient |
| Sub-object | from the dropdown. **`0000` is invalid. Codes starting `H` or `T` may not be used** |
| Activity Code, Function, Program/Phase | **from TheHub** |
| Commodity code | **81101500** Engineering Services · **84101604** Subrecipient agreements · anything else, ask Procurement |
| Line type | **Service** — all professional agreements |
| Buyer ID | always **`jameskr1`** |
| Requestor ID | per division |
| Shipping location | **SPSEE** — signifies the service details are in the agreement |
| Shipping method / Free on Board | **01** / **5** |
| Billing location | the division's own |
| Reporting tab | **2** Construction · **1** Unclassified |
| Discount | 1% — 0.00 · Days 0 · Disc Alw No |
| Terms & Conditions | one line: **ATTACH** |
| Vendor | Vendor Customer number from **VCUST** |
| Document header | Document Name, Budget FY, Description, Procurement Type, Effective Begin and Expiration dates |

Two notes. **Every commodity line carries an accounting line** — the two are
paired, not independent. And **Activity, Function and Program/Phase come from
TheHub**, which the portal already mirrors, so those are derivable rather than
typed.

**Caveat worth carrying:** Section 22 documents the **Engineering** path
(`Unit 0060`). Whether districts and other divisions key different fund, unit or
object values is not established — see Open-2.

**D-17 — Assembling the document is a defined manual sequence ending in a
drag-and-drop.** [PAG + Room]

Related Actions → Assemble Document → download the generated APO → **merge it with
the agreement PDF in Adobe or Bluebeam** → upload both back onto the header.

The friction was named in the room: *"the only way to get it uploaded in OASIS is
to use the console… you have to drag and drop."* Which is what prompted the ask in
Open-5.

**D-18 — The package handed to OASIS is black and white, and generated rather than
re-scanned.** [Room]

> *"Make sure it's in black and white, too. No color whatsoever. That'll get them
> rejected, too."*

And on quality: *"if you want it scanned and re-copied, and burned, and you get
fuzzies — which can happen sometimes when you scan too many times."* Produce the
PDF; don't put it through another scan generation.

## 5. Project numbers and coding

**D-19 — The Hub project key is what gets keyed into OASIS. The vendor's printed
number is a cross-reference.** [Room]

There is a person doing this by hand today:

> *"What I do is I have a little cheat sheet for those numbers that are popping up
> there that you wouldn't typically find in the hub. I have a cheat sheet so that
> when I enter them into OASIS."*

> *"That is not a hub number, but there is — you can go in the hub and find the
> actual hub number, and that's what I use, is the hub number, when I submit to
> OASIS."*

Determined direction, with agreement from the room: the system should do the
translation rather than a person. *"I'd rather have the system do it and have
nobody have to fix it."* Older jobs (2014-era numbers came up) may legitimately
have no match; for newer work the vendor can simply be told to use the right
number next time.

**D-20 — Participating vs non-participating is marked per payment line, and it can
change mid-agreement.** [Room]

> *"You have to put, like if it's participating, non-participating — you have to
> mark where the payment lines are and everything."*

> *"Sometimes there's multiple authorizations for the same project… in different
> phases, too. If one funding is full, the next funding — and it changes it from
> participating to non-participating. So it's just a back and forth."*

So participation is a property of the accounting line, not of the invoice, and one
project can carry several authorizations across phases.

**D-21 — Agreements do carry APO numbers, and getting them early would simplify
everything downstream.** [Room]

> *"If there was just a way to get them into OASIS first, then you would have that
> key. You'd have the APO number… and down the road, if you could bring it all
> together, you'll have it in the beginning."*

> *"I mean, I think agreements can have APO numbers, essentially. They do. In the
> system, too."*

This matters more than it sounds, because WVDOT's own master agreements carry no
printed agreement ID of their own — the OASIS document number is the most reliable
external key an invoice can be anchored to.

## 6. Explicitly out of scope

**D-22 — Construction contracts do not pass through this path.** [Room]

> *"We don't pay by invoice for our construction contracts, because we actually have
> — we say what we're going to pay… our inspector says they placed 50 tons of
> asphalt. We're the ones who say. They don't come to us and say, I want paid for
> 50 tons."*

Construction generates its own payment internally and interfaces **straight into
OASIS**, where an APO is created and then a PRC — an arrangement set up years ago.
It bypasses invoices entirely. The stated ambition is to get the same interface
treatment for purchase orders eventually, but that is a separate effort.
