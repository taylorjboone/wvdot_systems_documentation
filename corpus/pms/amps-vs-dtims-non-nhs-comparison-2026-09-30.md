## Findings (run 442)

1. **Spend matches.** Every year from 2026 to 2034 spends dTIMS's budget to within $0.4M. The total is **$18.37B against dTIMS's $18.57B**.
   - The unlimited 2035 buys $6.82B against $7.35B.
   - 2037 buys more ($682M against $305M): County Microsurface 5–7 years after the County Thick Overlays of 2030–31.
2. **Network condition tracks dTIMS, a few points better before 2035.**
   - Start (2025): 33.7 / 55.8 / 10.5 against 32.0 / 57.6 / 10.4.
   - 2028–2034: 4.8–5.3 points more Good.
   - 2035–2038: Good matches (57.9–58.2 against 57.4–57.5), and AMPS ends with about **2.9 points less Poor** (10.6 against 13.5).
3. **The extra Good is on surveyed road.** On dTIMS's surveyed sections AMPS starts 4.2 points higher (8.5% against 4.2%) and stays 6.7–7.8 points ahead through 2034. The no-data sections behave alike in both: Good until 2035, then Fair.
4. **Treatment mix now matches dTIMS on every large treatment (miles):**
   - Fair treatment 21,132 against 20,970 mi;
   - County Thick Overlay 1,471 against 1,561 mi;
   - Thick Overlay 3,649 against 4,111 mi;
   - Microsurfacing 1,502 against 1,873 mi.

   The visible difference is County Microsurface (800 against 255 mi), which follows the County Thick Overlays.
5. **Section by section, about 69% of dTIMS's treated miles get the same treatment from AMPS in the same year or within a year.**

   | dTIMS's treatment, as AMPS treats that section | % of miles |
   |---|---|
   | Same treatment, same year | 46.6% |
   | Same year, another treatment | 11.5% |
   | Same treatment, within ±1 year | 10.9% |
   | No work at all 2026–2038 | 8.5% |

   - Both systems treat the same 14,244 mi of sections. 1,398 mi are treated only by dTIMS and 1,338 mi only by AMPS. Of the miles dTIMS treats, AMPS also treats 91%.
   - The first treatment comes in the same year on 61% of shared miles and within a year on 80%. When they differ, AMPS is usually a year earlier (13%).
6. **What is left is the planning unit.**
   - AMPS plans joints (1.32 mi on average); dTIMS plans sections (2.33 mi), each spanning 1–5 joints.
   - Exact agreement falls with section length: 52% on 1–2 mi sections (1.3 joints each), 47% on 2–4 mi (1.8) and 39% on 4–8 mi (2.6).
   - AMPS can treat part of a long section. dTIMS's benefit isn't scaled by section length but its cost is, so it favours long sections.
   - The joint-level starting average also leaves more surveyed road Good than dTIMS's section average does.
7. **Microsurfacing timing is a selection choice.** Both place microsurfacing 5–7 years after an overlay. dTIMS mostly waits for the 7th year; AMPS also uses the 6th, so it lands in 2034 (1,093 mi against 518) rather than 2035.
8. **Section class agreement**: AMPS gives a section dTIMS's Good / Fair / Poor class on 91.7% of lane-miles at the start, 84.5–87.5% in 2027–2033, 81.3% in 2034 and about 90% from 2035.

## What was compared

| | AMPS run 442 | dTIMS |
|---|---|---|
| Network | the AMPS raw-survey segments on dTIMS's 11,676 analysis-set sections (view `dtims_reference_run_sections`) | DEL_STIP_2026_Non_NHS_ALL_NETWORK, 11,676 sections |
| Starting condition | config 336: joint-average start; County roads surveyed Nov–Dec 2024 counted as not delivered; unsurveyed road as dTIMS starts it; **patching from dTIMS's network** (pipeline stage `patching`) | dTIMS's own inventory |
| Optimizer | dTIMS strategies (whole-horizon strategies per joint, level 3, strategies to 2040, multi-year IBC) | dTIMS (level 3, analysis to 2040, IBC) |
| Budgets | $102M 2026, $3.5B 2027–28, $300M 2029–33, $1.5B 2034, unlimited 2035, $4B 2036–38 | the same |
| Committed work | the analysis's 221 committed treatments (imported into `projects`) | the same |

**Method.**
- The AMPS plan was regenerated per joint from run 442's stored inputs (its snapshot, config version and budgets). It reproduces the run's stored yearly results to 0.02 point.
- It was replayed on all 280,812 segments to get each segment's class each year. Each segment was then mapped to the dTIMS section it overlaps most.
- dTIMS's yearly class per section comes from replaying its selected program through its condition model, which reproduces its chart.
- Shares are by lane-miles unless stated otherwise.
- A section counts as treated by AMPS in a year when at least 50% of its length gets that treatment.
- Treatment mix by miles uses joint lengths (the run page merges touching joints into projects, so its miles differ slightly).

## Patching

- dTIMS's County Thick Overlay triggers on CCI < 1 with IRI > 150, **or on patching ≥ 15% with ADT ≥ 200**. 459 of dTIMS's 706 County Thick Overlays (985 mi) sit on sections that meet the patching branch.
- AMPS takes patching from dTIMS's stored network: pipeline stage `patching` fills `analysis_segments.patch_l/m/h` in place with the dTIMS section's patching, in proportion to segment length.
- The dTIMS-strategy optimizer sums patching over a joint.

## To close the remaining gap

- **Plan on dTIMS's sections instead of joints.** Use the stored sections as the elements, with one start per section from the section's survey average. That makes the planning unit and the starting average the same as dTIMS's. On dTIMS's own inventory the optimizer then reproduces dTIMS to 0.1 point and $18.56B.
- **Patching beyond dTIMS's network.** Patching now comes from dTIMS's stored network. The survey files also carry PATCH_L/M/H, which the pipeline could load directly for road outside dTIMS's network.
